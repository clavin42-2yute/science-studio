## Storyline

- **Phenomenon:** Photographs and a video show very large fallen trees supporting other things that live and grow in forests.
- **Question:** “How does a fallen tree become a nurse log?” Unit-driving question from local guide p. 50. The final class DQB umbrella question should use students' own language.
- **What this class has figured out:** Nothing reported yet. Beginning of the unit.
- **Evidence that starts the work:** Student observations of the publisher's images and fallen redwood video (slides B-H). The first explanations are tentative ideas, not facts to teach in advance.
- **Why the next activity follows:** Students model what they think happens on, under, and inside the fallen tree; compare models to identify shared ideas and uncertainties; connect to other related phenomena; then create a public question board and propose investigations. Lesson 2 follows by taking a closer look at a nurse log.
- **Teacher-reported questions or unresolved ideas:** None yet. Capture and preserve these when students contribute them; do not prefill the DQB.

## Learning and science

- **What students do/figure out now:** Develop initial models and questions from observations. By the end, students can begin to characterize nurse logs as fallen trees that help other things live and grow, and can distinguish visible observations from ideas about what may be in, under, or inside the log.
- **Three-dimensional learning goal (Teacher Guide p. 54):** SEP — Develop and use models. CCC — Scale, Proportion, and Quantity. DCI idea surfaced — matter and energy provided to other organisms. The model should show student thinking about size and changes over time while the class investigates how the fallen tree supports other organisms.
- **Unit performance expectations:** OpenSciEd identifies 5-PS1-1, 5-PS3-1, 5-LS1-1, and 5-LS2-1 as unit-level targets. Lesson 1's initial model is explicitly a pre-assessment; it does not establish mastery of these PEs. Do not score or grade it.
- **Plain-language teacher science:** A nurse log is a fallen tree that supports other life as a forest system changes over time. At this point, students should use what they can see and their experience to propose ideas. Do not supply the later explanation of matter or energy flows.
- **Left for later:** Which organisms are present and how components interact (Lesson 2); where plants get matter and energy (Lessons 3-7); how animals, decomposers, and other organisms transfer matter and energy (Lessons 8-15).
- **What today's evidence supports:** Observations of the images/video, student questions, and their starting models. It does not resolve how or why organisms live on/around the log, what is inside/under it, or how matter and energy move.

## Evidence checkpoint

- **Task/artifact:** Initial Model handout and contributions to the class Initial Consensus Model/DQB.
- **Assessment status:** Official pre-assessment, not scored/graded (Teacher Guide p. 55).
- **Look/listen for:** Drawings, words, speech, gestures or movement showing (1) what students think a nurse log gives other organisms, (2) arrows/relationships, (3) relative spatial scale, and (4) relative time for a fallen tree to become a nurse log.
- **Separate observation from interpretation:** Record direct observations (e.g., what is visible in image/video) separately from predictions (e.g., animals under log, roots through it). Do not interpret a misconception as failure; use it to identify the next evidence students need.
- **Response:** Preserve all initial ideas. Use uncertainty to shape Lesson 2's close observation. Avoid supplying lesson-later claims about matter/energy or correcting students' models before they encounter evidence.
- **Student record:** Keep teacher's formative notes unidentifiable or in approved school storage. Do not record named-student evidence in this generated planning package.

## Close and resume

- **At each stop:** Save the actual student-generated charts and note the last completed official component. Do not update the taught dashboard until teacher reports the actual lesson progress.
- **Final stop:** After Navigate, leave the DQB, initial consensus model, and related-phenomena chart visible/available. Preserve student questions and proposed investigations. The next official step is Lesson 2: look more closely at nurse-log components.
- **Classroom reflection after use:** One sentence on whether materials, timing or evidence need adjustment.

SCIENCE IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
Length, width and weight can rank the same objects differently. Weight lets students compare the amount
of matter in this classroom investigation. Measuring different pumpkins today helps students choose a
measure. To find out how much a plant grew, compare measurements of the same plant over time.

SET OUT
Prepare a Notice and Wonder chart, initial consensus model space, related phenomena chart and Driving
Question Board for each class. Gather sticky notes and markers. For Connect, stage six large sheets per
class for groups of three or four.
THE SCIENCE IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
A fallen tree can become a place where other organisms live and grow. The photos show what is visible on
or near a log. They leave questions about what is inside, how the log changes, and how it supports those
organisms. Save those explanations for later evidence.
PACING
Publisher sequence totals 110 minutes: Explore 30, Synthesize 30, Connect 20, Navigate 30. Use the
activity boundaries as checkpoints. If the 45-minute bell falls during an activity, preserve the work and
resume at the next unfinished slide. Teacher Guide printed pp. 50-73 covers the main lesson.

A / SET THE TASK
Open the Notice and Wonder handout or notebook T-chart. Ask students to record what they see and what

Possible observation: A tree appears to be growing out of the fallen trunk. Ask students to point to where it
meets the trunk.
C / MOSS ON THE LOG
Possible observation: There is a green covering. Ask which features they see before deciding what it is.
D / MUSHROOMS AND PLANTS
Ask students to describe two different things they notice. Invite a question about how either got there.
E / FALLEN TREE
Ask students to locate the small plant and compare it with the trunk.
F / PEOPLE FOR SCALE
Ask how the people help students estimate the size of the fallen tree.
G / PHOTO COLLECTION
Give students a second look. Ask what repeats across the five photos and what differs.
H / REDWOOD VIDEO
Play the clip and invite a new observation or question. If playback fails, return to the photos and gather
observations from those.
I / GROUP SHARE
Have students rehearse one observation and one wondering with their group.
J / CLASS CHART
Record students' observations and questions in their words.
K / DISCUSSION SUPPORT
Invite students to describe what they noticed and then explain what it made them think. Ask others to build
on or clarify an idea.

Introduce nurse log after students share. Save the chart. Resume with slide M if this is the stopping point.

Build the class model from students' ideas. Label visible features and use question marks for uncertain

If students suggest roots reach through the log, ask what they observed and what they predict. If ideas
conflict, record both and ask what a closer look could reveal.

Keep the models as an ungraded baseline. Listen for a relationship, an idea about relative size or change
over time, and an uncertainty the class could investigate. Resume with slide S after the model discussion.
